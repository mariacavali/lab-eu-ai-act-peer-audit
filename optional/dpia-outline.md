# MIRA — DPIA Outline

This outline supports an educational assessment; whether a DPIA is legally mandatory is **TBD — legal review**.

## Description of processing

MIRA collects user-supplied identity and birth details, business/creative free text, visual preferences, and optional inspiration or personal-reference images. It uses AI/model and infrastructure services to infer an internal Creative DNA and generate a five-frame visual campaign or moodboard. The controller, vendors, data flows, scale, locations, and any model-training reuse require confirmation.

## Necessity and proportionality

- Test each birth field and input against the core creative-direction purpose.
- Keep photographs optional and provide a viable non-upload route.
- Separate core generation from analytics, marketing, and model-improvement purposes.
- Define lawful bases, Article 9 handling, retention, user controls, and less intrusive alternatives.
- Assess whether expected benefits justify inference and vendor exposure.

## Risk identification

| Risk | Potential impact |
| --- | --- |
| Free text or imagery incidentally reveals Article 9 or third-party data | Unfair processing, exposure, distress, or loss of control |
| Creative DNA makes inaccurate, stereotyped, or sensitive inferences | Misrepresentation, discrimination, or user distress |
| Excessive inputs or indefinite retention | Increased breach impact and incompatible reuse |
| Multiple processors/subprocessors and non-EEA access | Reduced control, transfer risk, and difficult rights fulfilment |
| Deletion fails across derived data, caches, and backups | Persistent data after a valid erasure request |
| Weak transparency around AI and training reuse | Users cannot make informed choices or exercise rights |

## Mitigations

- Minimise required fields; filter or flag unnecessary sensitive submissions without inferring additional traits.
- Use upload warnings, third-party-content rules, and just-in-time notices.
- Prohibit unapproved training reuse; configure zero/limited retention where available.
- Execute Article 28 DPAs, verify subprocessors/transfers, and apply least-privilege security.
- Set purpose-specific deletion schedules and test access and erasure end to end.
- Allow users to review/correct profile inputs, regenerate outputs, and reach a human contact.
- Review risks and vendors at launch and after material changes.

## Outstanding evidence needed

- Controller identity, EU/EEA establishment, target users, age limits, and processing scale.
- Complete architecture/data-flow diagram and security controls.
- Exact vendors, roles, hosting regions, subprocessors, DPAs, and transfer mechanisms.
- Prompt/output logging, model-training use, analytics/cookies, and dataset-matching details.
- Retention/backup schedules and tested rights, deletion, and incident workflows.
